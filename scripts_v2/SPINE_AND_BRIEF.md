# Off the Clock v2: season spine and writing brief

Vertical (9:16) AI micro-drama, 10 episodes, Chicago. A spec pitch for Groupon. Lumen is the company (NOT Groupon).
Hunter Cole (early 30s) founded Lumen and is worth billions; he goes undercover on his own 4th floor as "Hunter from Ops".
Abby Reyes (late 20s, Mexican-American, Pilsen roots) is a product designer there: warm, teasing, money-mindful (never cheap,
never shamed for it). Tone: wholesome romance with real dramatic stakes. Groupon is the app that helps people enjoy fun things
without spending much (NOT an AI search). It appears on screen in Episodes 1, 3, 6, 8 and 10 only, naturally, never a hard sell.

## Why v2 exists
The user found v1 flat. v2 must have: intriguing openings, meaningful interactions, escalating tension, unexpected developments,
emotional turning points, carefully placed revelations, compelling cliffhangers; more playful banter; natural conversational
pacing with pauses and reactions; emotional variation; accurate speaker attribution; and INTERNAL MONOLOGUE throughout.

## The new engine (applies to every episode)
- **Ticking clock:** Episode 1 opens in the boardroom. Grant (COO, Hunter's rival) hands Hunter a 200-job restructure; Wave 1 is
  the 4th floor, Product Design: Abby's team. Hunter asked for "two weeks" and went undercover "to meet who I'm firing".
  The audience knows Abby's job is on his desk; Abby doesn't. Every episode should feel that clock.
- **Abby's stakes:** a promotion review she badly needs (she sends money home; her dad lost his job in a layoff when she was 12,
  revealed in Ep 5). She notices things: the watch, the cash, the dodges. She keeps a private count of his lies.
- **Grant hunts:** from Ep 2 he wonders where Hunter disappears to every day, and gets closer each episode.
- **Dramatic irony is the fuel:** let the audience know more than the characters, and let characters think one thing while saying another.

## Internal monologue rules (from the user; follow exactly)
- First-person voiceover of what a character thinks but does not say. Written as `NAME (V.O.): "..."` in the Dialogue column.
  V.O. never needs lips to move; spoken lines are `NAME: "..."` and are aimed at someone in the scene.
- Sound like real thoughts: natural, incomplete thoughts, questions, hesitation, sarcasm, humor, emotional reactions, observations.
  Never formal narration or documentary explanation. Keep each character's voice.
- Use it for what people wouldn't say aloud: attraction, suspicion, insecurity, regret, fear, ambition, hidden motives, conflicting loyalties.
- Multiple perspectives: mostly Hunter and Abby; occasionally Grant, Jo, Priya, Lupe, Celeste for irony. Do NOT reveal every thought;
  place them for dramatic irony, suspense, humor. A friendly exchange where both privately think different things is gold.
- Roughly 8-14 thoughts per episode. Thoughts often land in reaction shots or over action/inserts.

## Dialogue rules
Contractions, fragments, interruptions (—), overlaps, dodged questions, sarcasm, callbacks (the chalk/protein bar, "you're a project",
the lie count). Pauses and reactions are written into Picture ("a beat", "she bites back a smile"). Emotional variation inside each
scene. Nobody states the theme. Text messages lowercase. Light Chicago texture. Accurate attribution every time: every quoted line has a
speaker label; off-screen lines use `NAME (O.S.):`.

## Format (exactly like scripts_v2/ep01.md)
Heading `## Episode N: Title`, a **Stakes in one line**, a **Story in brief** (beginning / middle / end), then the table:
`| # | Scene | Shot | Sec | Picture | Dialogue / sound | Key |`
- Cut numbers 1, 2, 3 or 3a/3b for cuts within one shot. Sec is an integer, 2-6 (up to 8 for a deliberate long take).
- Runtime 95-120 seconds. Hook in the first 3 seconds. Episodes 2-9 open with a 2-4 second recap of the last cliffhanger.
- Pacing: spoken + thought words must stay under 2.6 words per second of the cut. Run `python check_script.py scripts_v2/epNN.md`
  from the production folder until it prints "pacing ok" and a runtime of 95-120.
- **Key column:** which existing keyframe the cut reuses, so we don't regenerate pictures needlessly:
  `eNN:S` = episode NN, shot S of the CURRENT production pack (see the `### Shot S · ...` headings and their Keyframe prompts in
  `packs_hand/epN.md`, or `packs_hand/ep1_plain.txt` for Episode 1); `plate:<location name>` = an empty set; or
  `new: <one-line image prompt>` when nothing existing fits. Prefer reuse; use `new:` only when the picture genuinely changes.
- Wardrobe and continuity follow the existing packs (Hunter's watch on the left wrist, Abby's mustard beanie and hoops, etc.).
